# Layer 2: Fundamental Analysis (Buffett Checklist)

> "It's far better to buy a wonderful company at a fair price
> than a fair company at a wonderful price." — Warren Buffett

## Purpose

Determine if this is a high-quality business with durable competitive
advantages — the kind Buffett would hold for decades.

## The Buffett Quality Checklist

### A. Economic Moat (경제적 해자)

The single most important concept. A moat protects profits from competition.

**Moat Types:**
| Moat Type | Description | Examples |
|-----------|-------------|---------|
| Brand Power | Pricing power from brand loyalty | Apple, Coca-Cola, Nike |
| Network Effects | More users = more value | Visa, Meta, Google |
| Switching Costs | Painful to leave | Microsoft Office, SAP, Adobe |
| Cost Advantage | Structural low-cost producer | Walmart, Costco |
| Intangible Assets | Patents, licenses, regulatory | Pfizer, Moody's |
| Efficient Scale | Market too small for 2 players | Waste Management, railroads |

**Assessment:**
- Wide Moat (🟢): Multiple moat sources, strengthening over time
- Narrow Moat (🟡): Some advantage but vulnerable
- No Moat (🔴): Commodity business, intense competition

### B. Profitability Metrics

These reveal whether the moat is translating into actual economic value.

**Must-Check Metrics:**

| Metric | Great | Good | Mediocre | Formula |
|--------|-------|------|----------|---------|
| ROE | > 20% | 15-20% | < 15% | Net Income / Shareholder Equity |
| ROIC | > 15% | 10-15% | < 10% | NOPAT / Invested Capital |
| Operating Margin | > 20% | 10-20% | < 10% | Operating Income / Revenue |
| Net Margin | > 15% | 8-15% | < 8% | Net Income / Revenue |
| FCF Margin | > 15% | 8-15% | < 8% | Free Cash Flow / Revenue |

**Buffett's Key Insight**: Look for CONSISTENCY. A company with 18% ROE
for 10 years is far better than one alternating between 30% and 5%.

- Check 5-year and 10-year averages, not just the latest quarter
- Watch for trends: improving, stable, or deteriorating?
- Compare to industry peers

### C. Financial Health

A great business shouldn't need excessive debt.

| Metric | Safe | Caution | Danger |
|--------|------|---------|--------|
| Debt/Equity | < 0.5 | 0.5-1.5 | > 1.5 |
| Interest Coverage | > 8x | 4-8x | < 4x |
| Current Ratio | > 1.5 | 1.0-1.5 | < 1.0 |
| Net Debt/EBITDA | < 2x | 2-4x | > 4x |

**Exceptions**: Some industries (banks, utilities, REITs) naturally
carry more debt. Compare within the same sector.

**Cash Flow Priority** (Buffett cares about this most):
1. Is operating cash flow consistently positive?
2. Is FCF consistently positive and growing?
3. Is the company self-funding its growth (FCF > CapEx needs)?
4. What's the FCF conversion ratio (FCF / Net Income > 80% is good)?

### D. Growth Quality

Not all growth is equal. Buffett values sustainable organic growth.

| Growth Type | Quality | What to Watch |
|-------------|---------|---------------|
| Organic revenue growth | High | Driven by volume + pricing power |
| Margin expansion | High | Operating leverage, efficiency |
| Acquisition-driven growth | Mixed | Integration risk, goodwill bloat |
| Debt-fueled growth | Low | Unsustainable, risky |

**Key Growth Metrics:**
- Revenue CAGR (5Y): > 10% is strong for large caps
- EPS CAGR (5Y): Should outpace revenue (operating leverage)
- FCF CAGR (5Y): The truest measure of value creation
- Dividend growth (if applicable): 10+ year growth streak is ideal

### E. Management Quality

> "I try to invest in businesses that are so wonderful that an idiot
> can run them. Because sooner or later, one will." — Warren Buffett

**Check for:**
- Insider ownership: Management has skin in the game?
- Capital allocation track record: Smart buybacks, M&A, dividends?
- Compensation: Aligned with long-term shareholder value?
- Candor: Does management acknowledge problems honestly in filings?
- Track record: Has the CEO delivered on previous guidance?

**Red Flags:**
- Excessive stock-based compensation diluting shareholders
- Empire-building acquisitions at high prices
- Changing accounting methods to inflate numbers
- CEO compensation growing faster than earnings
- Frequent "restructuring" or "one-time" charges

### F. Industry Position

- Market share: #1 or #2 in their segment?
- Industry structure: Oligopoly (good) vs fragmented (harder)?
- Secular tailwinds: Growing industry? (cloud, AI, aging population)
- Regulatory environment: Helps or hurts the moat?

## Scoring Rubric

🟢 **Strong Fundamentals** (3 points): Wide moat, ROE > 15% consistently,
   low debt, strong FCF, competent management, market leader

🟡 **Decent Fundamentals** (2 points): Some moat, acceptable profitability,
   manageable debt, mixed but improving metrics

🔴 **Weak Fundamentals** (1 point): No clear moat, inconsistent profitability,
   high debt, negative FCF, questionable management
